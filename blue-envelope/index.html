The Blue Envelope Project

Calmer stops. Clearer communication.

BEAA

What it is

A blue envelope holds the driver's license, registration, and insurance. It also carries short tips for the officer — whether it is the driver or a passenger who communicates differently.

Handing it over says: Someone in this car may need more time, and communicates differently.

Always voluntary. Not having one never means someone has no needs.

How it works

  1. 1
    Get an envelope

    From a participating Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV), police department, or advocacy group.

  2. 2
    Keep the vehicle's documents inside

    License, registration, and insurance, plus emergency contacts.

  3. 3
    Hand it over at a stop

    The officer reads the tips and adjusts how they communicate.

In short

This directory lists Blue Envelope programs by state, and by city or county where one is known. Some are required by state law, some are run by a state agency, and some exist only in specific places. Some states have none found yet.

Treat every entry here as a lead, not a confirmed fact. This directory was put together through research — state agency pages, legislature and bill-tracking sites, and news coverage — and most entries have not been confirmed by phone. A state marked "no program found yet" means nothing turned up in that research, not that no program exists. Call ahead before you rely on any address, phone number, or hours listed here, and tell us what you find if something has changed.

How to read this directory

Entries below often use short names for agencies: a Bureau of Motor Vehicles is a BMV, a Department of Public Safety is a DPS, a Police Department is a PD, and a Sheriff's Office is an SO. A local or county program is marked "matched to an official source" when this research found a government or agency page confirming it, or "news coverage only" when the only source found so far was a news report.

  • Required by state law — A state statute requires the program, so every driver in that state has the same guarantee.
  • Run statewide by a state agency — A state agency runs the program everywhere in the state, without a specific law requiring it.
  • Active in some cities or counties — One or more police departments, sheriffs, or counties run their own version. Coverage varies from place to place.
  • Proposed, not law yet — A bill has been introduced but has not passed.
  • No program found yet — Nothing turned up in this research. That is not a guarantee no program exists.

Arizona AZ Run statewide by a state agency

The Arizona Department of Transportation's Motor Vehicle Division runs the program statewide, working with the Department of Public Safety and the University of Arizona. It is voluntary and does not use a database.

Legal basis
A state agency partnership, not a specific law.
Started
2024–2025
Who it is for
Autistic drivers
How to get one
Any Arizona Department of Transportation Motor Vehicle Division office, or a partner agency.
Arkansas AR Required by state law

Arkansas runs two related programs passed as separate laws: a blue envelope (Act 16) for autistic drivers, and a green envelope (Act 531) for drivers with a mental illness.

Legal basis
Blue under Act 16; green under Act 531.
Started
Signed during the 2025 legislative session; both became available to drivers on January 2, 2026.
Who it is for
Blue: autism. Green: mental illness.
How to get one
Any of Arkansas's 130+ Revenue Offices, or by ordering online. No documentation is required.
California CA Active in some cities or counties

No statewide law exists — a bill, SB 664, has been introduced but not passed. San Diego County has run a program since October 2023, and San Bernardino and Riverside Counties (the Inland Empire) followed in 2025 and 2026.

Legal basis
No statewide law. Local programs run under their own county or city authority.
Started
San Diego: October 16, 2023. Inland Empire counties: 2025–2026.
Who it is for
San Diego: autism spectrum disorder, dementia, anxiety, and other conditions. Inland Empire: autism, intellectual and developmental disabilities, and deaf or hard-of-hearing drivers.
How to get one
San Diego: any participating sheriff's station or community partner, listed below. Elsewhere: a local regional center, police department, or sheriff's station.

Connecticut CT Required by state law

Connecticut's Department of Motor Vehicles ran the first Blue Envelope program in the country, starting in January 2020. Most other states' programs are modeled on it.

Legal basis
A public act of the Connecticut General Assembly.
Started
January 2020
Who it is for
Autistic drivers
How to get one
Connecticut Department of Motor Vehicles.
Delaware DE Run statewide by a state agency

Delaware State Police, the Delaware Chiefs of Police, the University of Delaware Center for Disabilities Studies, the DMV, and the Delaware Network for Excellence in Autism together run a statewide program.

Legal basis
A state initiative. A bill introduced in January 2026 would give it a stronger legal footing.
Started
Statewide, August 26, 2025
Who it is for
Autism and other disabilities affecting communication, sensory processing, or mobility
How to get one
Any Delaware State Police troop or participating agency. No identification is required.
Florida FL Required by state law

Florida's law requires the Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles to run an identification program, and requires law enforcement officer training on autism spectrum disorder. It builds on a program the Bal Harbour Police Department started locally.

Legal basis
Senate Bill 418, signed into law as Chapter 2026-147.
Started
Signed June 16, 2026; effective July 1, 2026.
Who it is for
Autism spectrum disorder
How to get one
Through the Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles, once the program is operational. Specific pickup locations were not yet published as of this research.

Louisiana LA Required by state law

Louisiana's law gives drivers a free green envelope (for a mental, physical, or developmental disability) or blue envelope (for autism) to hold their license and registration during a stop. Louisiana also has a separate Developmental Disability Disclosure Card program.

Legal basis
House Bill 590, signed into law as Act 816.
Started
Signed June 8, 2026; effective August 1, 2026.
Who it is for
Green: mental, physical, or developmental disabilities. Blue: autism.
How to get one
Free, no medical documentation required. The pickup process had not been detailed in coverage as of this research.

Ohio OH Run statewide by a state agency

Ohio's program runs through the state's county boards of developmental disabilities, and is live in 84 of the state's 88 counties. Each kit includes an envelope, a decal, a wallet card, and a brochure. The envelope design was licensed from the San Diego County Sheriff's Department. Be Enabled Advocacy Alliance is taking over coordination of the program statewide from the Southern Ohio Council of Governments (SOCOG) — see the waitlist on this page for updates.

State-supported, with no specific statute.

Developmental disabilities, including autism, anxiety, dementia, physical disability, and mental health conditions

Your county Board of Developmental Disabilities, free packet.

Federal and national efforts

Two related bills are moving through Congress, and several organizations coordinate programs above the state level.

  • Federal legislation — House

    Supporting Blue Envelope Programs Act (H.R. 6602)

    Introduced December 2025; in committee · Reps. Norma Torres (D-CA) and John Rutherford (R-FL)

    Would authorize Department of Justice grants for Blue Envelope programs. Has not had a floor vote as of this research.

  • Federal legislation — Senate

    Supporting Blue Envelope Programs Act (S. 4089)

    Introduced March 12, 2026; referred to the Judiciary Committee · Sens. Chris Coons (D-DE) and Eric Schmitt (R-MO)

    The Senate companion bill. Endorsed by the Autism Society, Autism Speaks, the Major County Sheriffs Association, the Fraternal Order of Police, Easterseals, The Arc, and NAMI, among others.

  • Federal appropriations

    FY2027 Commerce-Justice-Science Appropriations Report (House Report 119-652)

    House Appropriations Committee report

    Names Blue Envelope programs as an eligible use of Byrne Justice Assistance Grant funding, and directs the Department of Justice to notify grant recipients and report back to Congress.

  • National coordinator network

    National Public Safety Alliance for Individuals With Disabilities

    Active · Operated by Online Policing Solutions, Inc.

    Runs a chapter model across local, county, and state agencies. Describes the overall effort as decentralized — each agency implements its own version.

  • State sponsor — Maine

    Down Syndrome Advocacy Project of Maine

    Active · Erica Koch, ekoch@dsapmaine.org, (207) 754-5757

    Sponsors more than 30 Maine agencies with no state law behind it. Uses the broadest population description of any program.

  • Regional sponsor — California

    Autism Society Inland Empire and Inland Regional Center

    Active · San Bernardino and Riverside Counties

    Publishes a curriculum and best-practices guide, and encourages adding a health care passport to the envelope.

  • Regional sponsor — Illinois

    Autism Hero Project

    Active

    Sponsors programs at Bartlett, Harper College, Park Ridge, and other Illinois sites.

  • State sponsor — Oklahoma

    Active

    Partnered with the Metro Law Enforcement Leaders Network for the Oklahoma City-area rollout.

Where this directory comes from

This directory was put together by researching state agency websites, legislature and bill-tracking sites, and news coverage, including research assisted by AI tools. It has not been confirmed by phone with every program listed, and it was last checked in September 2026. Laws pass, programs launch, and contact details change faster than any directory can track on its own.

What "no program found yet" means

A Blue Envelope program is often started by a single police department or county, with no state registry recording it. That makes a small local program easy to miss. If you know of one that is missing from this directory, or a detail here that has changed, tell us what you found.
