Tell us your medical 911 story

You called 911, or someone called for you. What happened next, and what helped?

In short

Tell us about one time 911 was called for a medical emergency.

It can be good, bad, or mixed. Every story counts.

We also ask what helped, so we can share what works.

You do not need to give your name.

The story alone takes about 5 minutes. All of it takes about 15.

This is not a way to get help in an emergency.

If you need help right now, call 911. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988.

Why this topic first

When disabled people call 911, things can go wrong in ways other people never face.

  • A wheelchair or other equipment gets left behind.
  • Responders cannot understand how someone communicates.
  • Police come instead of, or along with, medical help.
  • A service animal is kept away.
  • Medicine or a device does not come to the hospital.

We hear these stories all the time. But almost nobody counts them.

We also want to know what helps. Many people keep a medical file at home, or sign up with their local 911 ahead of time. Nobody has asked whether these things actually work. You can tell us.

This connects to our Blue Envelope Project, which helps drivers share their needs during a traffic stop.

Your story

Every box tells you what it is for. There are 8 short parts. Only the story in part 2 is required.

Before you start

You must be 18 or older. A parent or caregiver can tell a child's story.

Only the story is required. Skip any question you like.

Please leave out names of people, hospitals, and streets.

A person on our team reads every story and removes anything that could point to you. Then we count it with everyone else's.

We never sell what you tell us.

Part 1 of 8: About the call

Whose story is this? (optional)

If it is someone else's story, answer every question about them.

Who called 911? (optional)
How did the call reach 911? (optional)
When did this happen? (optional)

A rough guess is fine.

How many times has 911 been called for you in the past year? (optional)

Count every medical call, not just this one.

Part 2 of 8: What happened

Start wherever you like. What was going on, who came, how they treated you, and how it ended. There is no wrong way to write it.

If someone only read one sentence of your story, what should it be?

Short on time? Skip to the end and send just your story

Part 3 of 8: Where it happened

So we can compare states.

We use it only to group stories by area and to compare them with public data, like income and how far the nearest hospital is. We never use it to find you. Leave it blank if you would rather.

What kind of place is it? (optional)
Where were you? (optional)

Part 4 of 8: How it went

Who came? (optional, choose all that apply)

Everyone who showed up, as far as you know.

How well did the 911 call-taker understand you? (optional)
How well did the responders understand your disability and needs? (optional)

Think about how they spoke to you, moved you, and listened.

How much did they include you in decisions about your care? (optional)
How safe did you feel with the responders? (optional)
Did any of these happen? (optional, choose all that apply)

These are things we hear about often.

If you could change one thing about how it went, what would it be?

Part 5 of 8: What helped

Many people prepare for emergencies ahead of time. Tell us what you had, and whether it helped. This is how we learn what works.

A medical information file at home, like a File of Life (optional)
A profile registered with 911 ahead of time, like Smart911 (optional)
A medical ID bracelet, wallet card, or phone medical ID (optional)
A communication card, board, or speech device (optional)
Someone with me who knew my needs (optional)
Telling the 911 call-taker about my disability (optional)

A tip for someone in your shoes. Something you did, said, or kept ready.

Part 6 of 8: Afterward

Did you go to the hospital? (optional)
Did you get a bill for the ambulance? (optional)

Count what you had to pay yourself, after insurance.

Before this happened, how did you feel about calling 911? (optional)
And now? (optional)
Overall, how did it go? (optional)

Part 7 of 8: About you

Only if you want to share. This shows us who is affected most, and whether some people are treated worse than others.

Which of these describe you? (optional, choose all that apply)

Use your own words in the next box if none of these fit.

How do you communicate? (optional, choose all that apply)
How old are you? (optional)
What is your race or ethnicity? (optional, choose all that apply)
What is your gender? (optional)

Part 8 of 8: How we may use your story

Can we share some of your words? (optional)

We always count your answers. This is only about quoting what you wrote, with nothing that names you.

Would you like to stay involved? (optional, choose all that apply)

Each of these needs an email address, so we can reach you.

Only if you ticked something above. We keep it apart from your story.

This is a preview. Sending is switched off until the campaign starts.

Your privacy

  • You do not need to give your name.
  • A person on our team removes anything that could point to you before your story is counted or quoted.
  • If you give an email address, we keep it apart from your story.
  • We never sell what you tell us.
  • If you gave an email address, you can ask us to delete your story at any time.
