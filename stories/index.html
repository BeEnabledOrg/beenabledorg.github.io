Your stories

One true story, in your own words. Together, they show what is really happening.

In short

We ask about one topic at a time. The first one is calling 911 for a medical emergency.

You can tell your story without giving your name.

We count what people tell us and share what we learn with everyone.

We use it to push for change, with facts that come from you.

This is not a way to get help in an emergency.

If you need help right now, call 911. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988.

Why we are asking

Officials often say they have no data on how disabled people are treated. Without numbers, our problems are easy to wave away as one-off stories.

One story is easy to ignore. Hundreds of stories that say the same thing are not.

So we ask the same short questions next to every story. That way we can count how often something happens, where it happens, and who it happens to.

We also ask what helped. Disabled people already know a lot about what works. Put together, that knowledge can help the next person.

What happens to your story

  1. 1
    You tell it

    Write as much or as little as you like. Only the story itself is required. Every other question can be skipped.

  2. 2
    A person reads it

    Someone on our team reads every story. We remove names, places, and anything else that could point to you.

  3. 3
    We count it

    Your short answers join everyone else's. That is how we find the patterns: what keeps going wrong, where, and what helps.

  4. 4
    We share what we learn

    We post what the stories show, here, for everyone. Only if you say yes, we may also share a few of your words, with nothing that names you.

  5. 5
    We take it to people who can fix it

    Lawmakers, agencies, hospitals, and emergency services. Facts from you carry more weight than anything we could say alone.
